# Configurational temperature: numerical verification of the draft's claims (2D φ⁴)

Scalar-field verification of the theoretical results and conjectures in the
`betaconfig/draft` manuscript, run on the 2D φ⁴ HMC ensembles
(`2Dphi4/trainingdata`, 10240 cfgs each) and the multi-L diffusion models.
All scripts live in `betaconfig/verification/`; figures in `figs/`.
Scripts were adversarially reviewed (physics + code, 8 independent reviewers)
before the production runs; all findings were fixed (see §Review).

**Conventions.** S = Σ_x[−2κ Σ_μ φ_x φ_{x+μ} + (1−2λ)φ_x² + λφ_x⁴], λ = 0.022,
β ≡ 1. Estimator β̂ = t₁ + t₂ with t₁ = TrH/g², t₂ = −2 gᵀHg/(g²)².
Forward noising is x_t = x₀ + σ_t ε in the normalised [−1,1] space of each model,
i.e. physical noise std s_t = c σ_t with c = (norm_max − norm_min)/2.

## A1+A2 — exact identity and volume scaling of the directional term

Script `a2_term_scaling.py`, figure `figs/a2_term_scaling.pdf`, data `a2_terms.csv`.

Raw HMC, full estimator ⟨β̂⟩ = ⟨t₁+t₂⟩ (blocked SEM, 64 blocks):
all 20 (κ, L) combinations agree with 1 within |z| ≤ 2.1. Examples at κ=0.28:
1.00048(224) [L=8] … 0.99991(13) [L=128].

Volume scaling of the directional term, raw HMC log-log fit of −⟨t₂⟩ vs L
(L = 8…128 with 10240 cfgs; L = 256 added later with 5120 cfgs freshly
generated by `generate_hmc_L256.jl` — Wolff+FAHMC, n_wolff=5, thin=5 so stored
configs are ~independent, τ_int(|m|) ≤ 0.36 stored cfgs, 8 FA metric refits,
doubled warmup near κ_c):

| κ | 0.26 | 0.2705 | 0.27088 | 0.28 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| slope (L≤128) | −2.008 | −2.005 | −2.004 | −2.007 |
| slope (L≤256) | −2.006 | −2.004 | — | −2.005 |

At L=256 (extended to 10240 cfgs by continuing each chain from its last stored
configuration, `extend_hmc_L256.jl`; old/new segments agree in ⟨φ²⟩ to 5
digits, splice-free power spectra): ⟨β̂⟩ = 0.99984(8), 1.00011(7), 1.00024(7)
for κ = 0.26, 0.2705, 0.28. The κ=0.28 value sits 3.5σ above 1 — with ~40
identity tests run in this project a single 3.5σ outlier is unremarkable
(look-elsewhere ≈ 2%), but worth re-checking if it persists with more data.

i.e. t₂ ∝ 1/V with per-mille accuracy in the exponent, **independently of the
phase** (symmetric, near-critical, broken). The same holds on noised ensembles
(s = 0.48, 4.8; slopes −2 within errors, see figure panel 2). −⟨t₂⟩ is nearly
κ-independent at fixed L (≈ 0.043 at L=8 → 1.6·10⁻⁴ at L=128).

Practical consequence for the paper: the t₁-only (diagonal-Hessian) estimator
used at U(2)/SU(3) carries a bias −⟨t₂⟩ ≈ −0.04/(L/8)² in 2D φ⁴ — negligible at
L ≳ 64, but the *full* estimator is exact at every V, so the 2D scalar section
can quote it as the clean baseline for the finite-size effect shown in
`u2_finite_size_beta12`.

## B1 — large-noise asymptotics and the quartic tail

Script `b1_gaussian_tail.py`, figure `figs/b1_gaussian_tail.pdf`.

Treating the noised field as Gaussian with the measured 2-pt moments
(G₀, G₁, G₂, G_d; white noise only shifts G₀ → G₀ + s_t²), Wick gives

- E[TrH]/site = 2a + 12λG₀,  a = 1−2λ
- E[g²]/site = 4κ²(4G₀+4G₂+8G_d) + 4a²G₀ + 240λ²G₀³ − 32κaG₁ − 192κλG₀G₁ + 48aλG₀²

and β_conf ≈ E[TrH]/E[g²] up to O(1/V) (checked: the mean-of-ratios shift is
+2.1% at V=32², +0.3% at s=2 — a real 1/V effect from Var(g²) ∼ 45/V at large s).

Results (κ=0.28, L=64 and 128, measured forward curves from
`results/beta_conf_traj_multiL`):

- the Wick-Gaussian curve reproduces the measured β_conf(s_t) over **11 decades**;
- the asymptotic tail is β_conf → 1/(20λ G₀(t)²) ∝ s_t⁻⁴ — the quartic term
  dominates g. The local log-slope passes −2 only transiently and settles at −4.
  **The draft's Gaussian-example scaling σ₀²/σ_t² is the quadratic-action special
  case; for any interacting (quartic) scalar theory the deep-noise tail is −4.**
  Worth one sentence in the draft;
- measured/Wick → 1.45 as s_t → 0: the broken-phase target is strongly
  non-Gaussian, and the ratio quantifies exactly how much of β_conf's approach to
  1 is *not* captured by 2-pt information. The iid-Wick curve (no neighbour
  correlations) is off by ×8.7 at small s_t — neighbour correlations matter.

## B2 — Gaussian example: finite-N statements (draft to-do, l.403)

Script `b2_gaussian_example.py`, figure `figs/b2_gaussian_example.pdf`.
Pure numpy; μ₀=1.5, σ₀=0.8.

The draft's α-integral has the numerically stable series form
I = ½ Σ_n Pois(n; z)/(N/2−1+n), z = NΔμ_t²/(2σ_t²), used throughout.

- **VE**: β_conf(t) = σ₀²/(σ₀²+t) is **exact at every N > 2** (N=3,4,8,64
  checked; MC z-scores ≤ 2.4). Reason: all 1/N corrections carry λ_t² and
  λ_t ∝ Δμ_t = 0 in VE. The draft's "large-N" derivation is stronger than
  advertised for VE.
- **VP** (t=1, λ_t = 0.2865): quadrature vs MC agree (z ≤ 0.7);
  N(β_N/β_∞ − 1) → −0.1641 vs predicted −2λ_t² = −0.1641 — the 1/N coefficient
  of the draft is verified to 4 digits.
- Caveat worth knowing: the MC estimator of ⟨1/‖x−μ₀‖²⟩ has **infinite variance
  for N ≤ 4** (⟨1/‖·‖⁴⟩ diverges); N=3,4 must be handled analytically.

## B3 — free-field (anisotropic) generalisation — new analytic result

Script `b3_free_field.py`, figure `figs/b3_free_field.pdf`.

For S = ½φᵀMφ and VE noising (Cov = M⁻¹ + τ·1):

  β_conf(τ) = TrM / (TrM + τ TrM²) + O(1/V) = 1/(1 + τ ⟨ω²⟩/⟨ω⟩),

with ω_k = 2 − 4κ(cos k₁ + cos k₂) on the lattice; reduces to the draft's
σ₀²/(σ₀²+τ) for M = 1/σ₀²·1. Derivation: g = Mφ, H = M ⇒
E[g²] = Tr(M²C_τ) = TrM + τTrM², E[TrH] = TrM; self-averaging does the rest.

Verified with exact Gaussian sampling (FFT-filtered white noise), κ=0.20:
τ=0 gives β_conf = 0.99991(150) [L=16], 0.99977(36) [L=64];
max |measured/formula − 1| over τ ∈ [10⁻³, 10³] at L=64: **0.07%**.
V·⟨t₂⟩ collapses for L=16 vs 64 along the whole flow (t₂ ∝ 1/V at every τ).
This is a candidate short addition to the draft's analytical-example appendix.

## C4 — Z₂ sector collapse is exactly invisible: a boundary of the diagnostic

Script `c4_z2_blindness.py`, figure `figs/c4_z2_blindness.pdf`.

g is odd and H even under φ → −φ, so β̂(φ) = β̂(−φ) identically. Demonstrated at
κ=0.28, L=64 (deep broken phase, ⟨|m|⟩ = 1.456): projecting the Wolff-symmetric
ensemble onto one sector changes ⟨m⟩ from −0.009 to +1.456 and the susceptibility
from χ = 8682 to χ_c = 4.1 (three orders of magnitude), while the *per-config*
β̂ values are unchanged to machine precision (max diff 5.6·10⁻¹⁶, the residue of
the affine normalisation round-trip; bypassing the map it is bitwise zero).

Consequence for the draft: the introduction's "sensitive to metastability"
(citing Dhindsa et al.) needs a qualifier — **ergodicity violations that are
symmetries of the action are exactly invisible** to β_conf. A mode-collapsed
generative model producing one Z₂ sector would pass the thermometer perfectly.
This complements the locality limitation observed for U(2) extrapolation.

## C1 — locality conjecture (near-critical epoch scan)

Script `c1_epoch_locality.py`, figure `figs/c1_epoch_locality.pdf`,
data `c1_results.json`. Multi-L κ=0.2705 run (σ=360), L=64, 16 log-spaced
epochs, N=512 samples each (EM, 2000 log steps). HMC reference (connected):
χ_c = 1686(11), ξ = 11.2(1), ⟨φ²⟩ = 1.4388.

Findings:

- ep ≤ 57: model produces noise-like fields; β_conf = 0.26–0.52 flags it at >40σ.
- ep 108–201: sharp transition; β_conf = 0.877 → 0.906 while ⟨φ²⟩ is 19% → 11%
  off, χ 60% → 16% off — **all estimators fire together**.
- ep ≥ 376: χ, ξ, F(k_min) sit at their statistical floors (2–5% at N=512);
  β_conf keeps resolving a residual −0.6% → −0.3% deviation
  (0.9813(18) → 0.9942(12)), far above its own 0.1% floor.

**Verdict on the locality conjecture: not reproduced in this setting.** There is
no epoch where β_conf ≈ 1 within errors while a long-range observable shows a
resolved deviation; the failure ordering is the opposite — at equal sample
budget the thermometer is uniformly the *most* sensitive probe of undertraining.
Two caveats keep the U(2) observation compatible: (i) our χ/ξ floors (~3–5%)
cannot exclude a silent IR-only defect below that size; (ii) the U(2) failure
arose from *score-rescaling extrapolation*, a different error mode than
undertraining — the scalar analogue of that mode is the C3 family test below,
which does show large IR-blind discrepancies. Note the late-epoch β_conf
plateau contains the known −6·10⁻⁴ ε-smearing bias (band in the figure).

## C2 — sensitivity to sampler quality

Script `c2_sensitivity.py`, figure `figs/c2_sensitivity.pdf`,
data `c2_results.json`. Multi-L κ=0.28 model, ep 10000, L=64, N=512; the
sampler output is the production one (final mean_x, no trailing noise kick).

Matched step grid N_t ∈ {50…5000} for both schedules:

| N_t | log β_conf | linear β_conf | ⟨φ²⟩ log / linear (ref 2.6892) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 0.0000(0) | 1.5674(56) | 1.6·10⁶ / 2.5275 |
| 100 | 0.0000(0) | 1.3282(25) | 2.2·10⁵ / 2.5785 |
| 200 | **0.9410(13)** | 1.1728(16) | 2.6905 / 2.6320 |
| 350 | 0.9752(13) | 1.0992(14) | 2.6859 / 2.6477 |
| 500 | 0.9823(12) | 1.0673(14) | 2.6936 / 2.6624 |
| 700 | 0.9881(12) | 1.0472(13) | 2.6924 / 2.6717 |
| 1000 | 0.9949(12) | 1.0322(13) | 2.6871 / 2.6712 |
| 1400 | 0.9938(11) | 1.0269(13) | 2.6844 / 2.6776 |
| 2000 | 0.9987(13) | 1.0154(13) | 2.6906 / 2.6838 |
| 3000 | 0.9992(12) | 1.0108(12) | 2.6888 / 2.6812 |
| 5000 | 0.9994(12) | 1.0095(13) | 2.6802 / 2.6762 |

The two schedules converge to unity from opposite sides — log from below
(residual noise), linear from above (over-smoothed final mean_x at the
effective cutoff t_eff ~ 1/N_t) — and the linear grid retains a percent-level
bias even at N_t = 5000.

- log/200 is the headline: ⟨φ²⟩ agrees to 0.05% and χ within its ~3% error,
  yet β_conf = 0.941 — a **45σ** detection of the discretisation bias. At equal
  sample budget the thermometer resolves sampler bias one to two orders of
  magnitude below the observable floors.
- linear schedules now *overshoot* (β_conf > 1): with the trailing kick removed,
  the dominant artefact is the over-smoothed final mean_x at the effective
  cutoff t_eff ~ 1/steps (too-narrow ensemble → smaller g² → β̂ > 1). Direction
  of the β_conf deviation distinguishes "residual noise" (β<1) from
  "over-denoising" (β>1). Monotone convergence 0.941→0.982→0.995→0.9987 with
  log steps.

## C3 — quasi-equilibrium hypothesis p_t ≈ e^{−β_conf(t)S}

Script `c3_quasi_equilibrium.py`, figure `figs/c3_quasi_equilibrium.pdf`,
data `c3_results.json`. κ=0.28, L=64.

**A — family test** (final version: Wolff+FAHMC Boltzmann ensembles). The
Boltzmann side e^{−β_fwd(σ_t)S} is sampled by mapping βS_{κ,λ} to the standard
action via x = c·φ (κ' = βκc², λ' = βλc⁴, 2βλc⁴ + β(1−2λ)c² = 1) and running
the exact Wolff+FAHMC cluster sampler (`crosscheck_c3_wolff.jl`), 13 σ_t
points, 4096 cfgs each; ⟨β̂⟩ = β holds on every ensemble (|z| ≤ 1.8, blocked).

| σ_t | β_fwd | χ_c (true p_t) | χ_c (e^{−βS}) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.02 | 0.9704 | 4.0 | 4.3 |
| 0.05 | 0.8398 | 4.1 | 9.2 |
| **0.07** | 0.7264 | 4.2 | **167.8** |
| 0.10 | 0.5620 | 4.3 | 30.8 |
| 0.20 | 0.2287 | 4.9 | 6.7 |
| 1.00 | 0.0033 | 26.6 | 15.8 |

**The quasi-equilibrium family fails by a factor ~40 at the flow's crossing of
the effective critical line** (σ_t ≈ 0.07, where β_fwd·S sits closest to
criticality): matched β_conf, χ_c different by 167.8/4.2. One number cannot
pin down the IR content of p_t. This is the scalar, fully quantitative version
of the U(2) locality caveat — and directly bounds the accuracy of the "Wilson
action with β_conf(t) as drift" trick.

**Methodological postscript (MALA episode).** The first version of this test
used vectorised MALA with warm starts and a dual-chain-set IR gate. An
independent Wolff cross-check showed the MALA ensembles were IR-under-relaxed
near the critical crossing (χ_c = 33.6 vs 174 at β = 0.726, z ≈ 16–19): both
chain sets inherited the warm start's IR structure, so the gate was blind to
the shared bias, while the ⟨β̂⟩ = β gate passed throughout — an empirical
demonstration that the thermometer is UV-local and cannot certify IR
equilibration. All C3-A numbers now come from the cluster ensembles
(`c3_wolff.json`); part B (score-free annealing) never used MALA.

**B — score-free annealed sampler** (drift −β_fwd(σ_t)·c·g, EM 2000 log steps):
final ensemble has β_conf = 0.731, ⟨φ²⟩ = 1.78 (−34%), χ = 13 (vs 8682) — it
fails to rebuild the condensate. Consistent with A: in a broken/near-critical
phase the quasi-equilibrium drift is not a substitute for the learned score
(the U(2) success at β=2 lives in a single-phase, weakly-correlated regime).
Notably β_conf itself flags the failure (0.731), so the diagnostic correctly
polices its own approximation.

## Review

Scripts were reviewed adversarially (8 independent agents, physics + code)
before production runs. Findings, all fixed:

- **critical** `common.py action_phys`: missing μ=2 hopping term (would have made
  C3's MALA sample an anisotropic wrong theory). Fixed; a `torch.autograd`
  cross-check of grad vs action is now asserted in C3.
- **critical** `c4` bitwise assert: the affine map inside `phi4_beta_conf` breaks
  exact sign symmetry at the ulp level; assert relaxed to 10⁻¹², wording fixed.
- **major** `em_reverse` returned the state *after* the final noise kick, unlike
  the production sampler (returns mean_x). For linear schedules the last kick
  alone imposes a floor |β_conf−1| ≈ 0.04–0.14 that no score can beat. Fixed to
  return mean_x.
- **major** ξ was computed from the disconnected χ = V⟨m²⟩ — meaningless in the
  broken phase (condensate-dominated). Now ξ uses χ_c = V(⟨m²⟩−⟨|m|⟩²).
- **major** C3 equilibration gate was circular (warm start from the compared
  ensemble; β̂ is UV-local and blind to IR relaxation). Now: disjoint
  init/measurement sets, warm + overdispersed (×1.5) chain sets, and an IR
  agreement gate on χ_c and F(k_min) between the two sets.
- **major** statistical noise floors added to C1/C2 comparisons (χ, ξ have ~3–5%
  floors at N=512; β_conf resolves 0.1%); error bars added throughout.
- **minor** A2 identity panel switched to z-scores; ε-smearing bias of the
  reverse endpoint (−6·10⁻⁴ at ε=10⁻⁵) marked as a band in C1.
